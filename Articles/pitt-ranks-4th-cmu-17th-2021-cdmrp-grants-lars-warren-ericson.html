In my previous article I attempted to use Google Bard to rank universities by reseach grant income. Alas, I suspect the results were nonsense. None of the GPTs can really access a relevant database or make sense of it. The work needs to be done by hand. Also, it turns out that apparently there is no single go-to place for this kind of scoring. It is necessary to pick a proxy index and do the work by hand.

I care about biotech, as it relates to treatments for NF1. A relevant proxy index for NF1 research, and potential capacity of a research institution to do effective and relevant NF1 research if they don't do it already, is the Congressionally Directed Medical Research Programs. They provide a handy index which makes it easy to compare institutions based on the CDMRP grant income, say for 2021, which is the most recent year with a full set of records.

So I did the download and discovered that my alma mater #CMU ranks well below it's nearest neighbor #Pitt in 2021 CDMRP grants. Pitt is near the top of the ranks. Above Pitt is Stanford at #3, UCSF and Ohio State at #2 and Johns Hopkins at #1. Harvard (MGH) is 6th. CMU's peers are Thomas Jefferson U., the U. of Queensland, Uniformed Services U. and BU.

One story the table tells is that Ivy League has very little to do with it: The talent lies where the talent lies. In medical research, CMU is on the list but way down at the bottom. Just saying. CMU is not much of a player in the world of medical research, which I consider a pretty good proxy for saying it is not a player in biotech. In the comments below, find another proxy, do the same number crunching, and prove me wrong!
