Using data from this query on USASpending.gov, following a suggestion from William Walker III, we can rank the top STEM universities based on 2024 overall US Government research income, in four broad non-social science research areas. I did this because I'm trying to recommend colleges for my son to apply to.

Biotechnology

The top name is a medical school. The next two are a bit of a surprise to me: BU and Duke. Johns Hopkins I expect to see. Some of these are mixed up because Harvard College and President and Fellows of Harvard are two different entities. Normalizing the names is too much effort. The big surprise takeaway is that if you want to do biotech, you might be better off at BU than at Harvard.

Nanotechnology

If you want to work in Nanotechnology, and who doesn't (actually: who does?), this is a surprisingly narrow field, with only two places to go, my Mom's alma mater Wash. U in St Louis, and MIT. The big surprise here is that you are far, far better off at Wash. U than at MIT:

Physical, engineering, and life sciences

This is a broader category in which Wash. U slips down to 45th place. MIT is still 2nd place! CalTech is #1. I will show you the top 20. This one skews massively towards CalTech. I thought CalTech was a small school. Then MIT, then Johns Hopkins, and a surprise of CMU in 4th place. The take drops down rapidly from there. The two heavy hitters, CalTech and MIT, are in a weight class by themselves.

Space research and technology

Space is another niche where BU does well, but not as well as BC! Apparently CalTech doesn't do this stuff. Who knew?

Conclusions

BU is a surprise standout in these rankings in several niche categories. In the broadest ranking, BU is still a top 10 player. Respect! But MIT and CalTech are the stone-cold heavyweights.
