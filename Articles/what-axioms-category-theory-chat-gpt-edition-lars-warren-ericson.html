The axioms of classical logic and setoids are foundational principles that underpin reasoning in these respective frameworks. Here's a brief overview of the axioms for each:

Axioms of Classical Logic:

  2. Law of Non-Contradiction (LNC): A proposition cannot be both true and false simultaneously. Symbolically, this is expressed as ¬(P ∧ ¬P).

These axioms reflect the principle of bivalence, which states that every proposition must be either true or false, and there are no other truth values.

Axioms of Setoids (Equivalence Relations):

  1. Reflexivity: Every element is related to itself. For every element x, the relation R is reflexive if R(x, x) holds.
  2. Symmetry: If two elements x and y are related, then y and x are also related. For every elements x and y, the relation R is symmetric if R(x, y) implies R(y, x).
  3. Transitivity: If two elements x and y are related, and y and z are related, then x and z are also related. For every elements x, y, and z, the relation R is transitive if R(x, y) and R(y, z) imply R(x, z).

These axioms define the properties of an equivalence relation, which is a binary relation that exhibits reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity. Setoids are used in Coq to provide a foundation for reasoning about relations, allowing for precise manipulation of equivalence classes and related concepts.
