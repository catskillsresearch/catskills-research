PAC-3 ABM supply chain and economics

Created on 2026-04-13 13:11

Published on 2026-04-13 14:07

PAC-3 is an antiballistic missile in frequent use in Ukraine and the Middle East. It is manufactured by RTX, which is the prime contractor for the launching system, Lockheed Martin, which is the prime contractor for the interceptor, and parts by Boeing, L3Harris Technologies and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries.

One PAC-3 interceptor, not including launch platform and its radar, costs US$5,000,000.

It takes 3 years to source the parts.

The parts come from 8 countries: the United States, Japan, Germany, Poland, the UK, the Netherlands, Taiwan, South Korea, Australia, Canada, and China.

It takes 3 months after parts are sourced to manufacture one PAC-3 interceptor.

PAC-3's have been fired in Ukraine against Russian missiles and in the Middle East against Iranian missiles. In the case of Zircon and Kinzhal missiles, this was cost-effective. In the case of Iskander-M and Fattah, break-even. In the case of Kh-101, Emad and Shahed, a net loss.

Russia launched Oreshnik IRBMs with MIRV warheads at Ukraine two times, once in 2024 and 2026. In both cases, Ukraine had PAC-3 and SAMP/T interceptors available. These were not deployed because the MIRV bus overwhelms them. Ukraine doesn't currently have Oreshnik-capable interceptors. The ones that might work are US Aegis SM-3, US THAAD and Israel Arrow 3.
