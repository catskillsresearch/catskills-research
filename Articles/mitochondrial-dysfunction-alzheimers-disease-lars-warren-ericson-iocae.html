Research

Mitochondrial dysfunction plays a fundamental role in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Several recent studies have highlighted the importance of mitochondrial abnormalities in the development and progression of AD:

  1. Mitochondrial dysfunction, including disruption of the electron transport chain, changes in mitochondrial dynamics, calcium signaling, and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, is a key feature of AD pathology.1234

  2. Damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria can lead to reduced ATP production, calcium dysregulation, and oxidative stress, all of which contribute to neuronal dysfunction and death in AD.45

  3. Accumulation of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides can directly impair mitochondrial function, causing mitochondrial structural abnormalities, fission-fusion imbalance, and synaptic damage.14

  4. Mitophagy, the selective degradation of damaged mitochondria, is also impaired in AD, leading to the accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria.4

  5. Restoring mitochondrial function through interventions like physical exercise, antioxidant diets, or targeted therapies may help delay the onset and slow the progression of AD.4

In summary, the search results indicate that mitochondrial dysfunction is a central mechanism underlying the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease, and targeting mitochondrial pathways represents a promising avenue for the development of future AD treatments.12345

Drug therapies

  1. There are a few drugs currently under clinical evaluation for treating mitochondrial dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease:

  • Sulforaphane (NCT03630419) - A compound that influences inflammatory pathways. The study is estimated to be completed by December 2022.2

  • CP2 - A drug that decreased amyloid-beta and tau levels, and improved mitochondrial bioenergetics in preclinical studies.2

  • AP39 - A hydrogen-sulfide donor that increased ATP levels, prevented mtDNA damage, reduced ROS, and prevented brain atrophy in animal models.2

  • Mdivi-1 - A mitochondrial division inhibitor that prevented mitochondrial fragmentation, loss of membrane potential, and ROS generation, while also enhancing memory and learning in preclinical studies.2

  1. However, the search results note that there are significant challenges in developing drugs targeting mitochondrial dysfunction for Alzheimer's disease:

  • Difficulty in accurately diagnosing the extent of mitochondrial dysfunction and determining the appropriate therapeutic dose.2

  • Lack of validated, sensitive and specific biomarkers to enable effective drug design and formulation.2

  • Mitochondrial dysfunction is implicated in many other diseases beyond just Alzheimer's, which complicates drug development.2

Charities

  1. The Michael J. Fox Foundation for Parkinson's Research supports research on mitochondrial dysfunction, as it is a key driver of both Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases. They have funded studies on developing mitochondrial modulators as potential treatments.1

  2. The BrightFocus Foundation, through its Alzheimer's Disease Research program, has awarded over $1.3 million in grants in 2023 to support research exploring the role of mitochondrial dysfunction in Alzheimer's and other neurodegenerative diseases.2

  3. The IDSA (Infectious Diseases Society of America) Foundation established the Microbial Pathogenesis in Alzheimer's Disease Grant program in 2018 to foster research investigating a potential microbial link to Alzheimer's, including the role of mitochondrial dysfunction.5

  4. The Benter Foundation believes that innovative scientific research is key to winning the battle against Alzheimer's disease, and has supported the IDSA Foundation's grant program exploring microbial and mitochondrial mechanisms.5
