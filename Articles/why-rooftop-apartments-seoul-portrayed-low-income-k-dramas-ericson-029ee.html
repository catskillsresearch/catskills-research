Why are rooftop apartments in Seoul portrayed as low income dwellings in K-dramas?

The portrayal of rooftop apartments as low-income dwellings in K-dramas reflects reality in several ways:

Cost: Compared to standard apartments in bustling Seoul, rooftop apartments are generally much cheaper. This is because they often lack air conditioning, proper insulation, and other amenities considered standard in more expensive housing. They can also be smaller and have less sunlight due to their location. So, for individuals or families struggling financially, rooftop apartments offer a more affordable option.

History: Historically, rooftop apartments were originally built as storage units or caretaker quarters. Over time, as Seoul's housing costs soared, these spaces were converted into makeshift living quarters for people who couldn't afford standard apartments. This connection to past struggles continues to influence their portrayal in media.

Duality: While being associated with financial limitations, rooftop apartments can also offer unique advantages. They often have balconies or rooftop access, providing city views and a sense of openness. This creates a contrast in K-dramas, where characters living in cramped, hot conditions still have access to a glimpse of the vibrant city and its possibilities.

Symbolic storytelling: K-dramas often focus on themes of overcoming adversity and chasing dreams. By placing characters in these humble dwellings, writers can illustrate their resilience and determination to succeed despite their circumstances. It becomes a visual metaphor for their struggles and aspirations.

Of course, K-dramas also take creative liberties: Not all rooftop apartments are depicted as exclusively for low-income individuals. Some dramas portray them as trendy or unique options for young people or artists, highlighting their distinct character and potential for creative expression.

Overall, the portrayal of rooftop apartments in K-dramas reflects a complex reality shaped by economic realities, historical context, and creative storytelling choices. While they certainly represent the challenges faced by low-income individuals in Seoul, they can also symbolize hope, resilience, and the unique urban spaces that contribute to the city's character.
