What is the "systems movement"?

The "systems movement" refers to a broad intellectual and interdisciplinary trend that emerged in the mid-20th century, focusing on the study of complex systems and their interactions. It encompasses various fields, including:

  1. Systems theory (e.g., Ludwig von Bertalanffy's General Systems Theory)

  2. Cybernetics (e.g., Norbert Wiener's work on feedback and control)

  3. Systems dynamics (e.g., Jay Forrester's work on modeling complex systems)

  4. Chaos theory and complexity science

  5. Ecological and environmental systems thinking

  6. Social systems theory (e.g., Niklas Luhmann's work on social systems)

The systems movement seeks to understand complex phenomena by analyzing the relationships, interactions, and interdependencies within and between systems. It often emphasizes a holistic, interdisciplinary approach, recognizing that systems exhibit properties that emerge from the interactions of their components, rather than being reducible to those components alone.

This movement has influenced various domains, including science, philosophy, sociology, ecology, economics, management, and more. It continues to shape our understanding of complex systems and informs approaches to problem-solving, decision-making, and sustainability.

  • Paradigmatic shifts: The rise of new intellectual trends, such as postmodernism, poststructuralism, and critical theory, shifted attention away from systems thinking. These perspectives emphasized the fragmented nature of reality, challenging the idea of coherent systems.
