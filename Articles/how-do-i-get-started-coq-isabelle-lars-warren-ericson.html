What is the difference between Coq and Isabelle?

Coq and Isabelle are both popular proof assistants used for formal verification and proof development, but they have some key differences in their underlying principles and design:

Logical Foundations:

  • Coq is based on a variant of the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC), which combines dependent types, higher-order logic, and inductive definitions. It allows for the formalization of mathematics and the development of certified software.
  • Isabelle, on the other hand, is based on a variant of higher-order logic called Isabelle/HOL. It provides a generic framework for formal reasoning and supports various logics, including first-order logic, higher-order logic, and set theory.

Proof Styles:

  • Coq emphasizes the use of interactive and tactic-based proof development. It provides a scripting language where users construct proofs by composing tactics, which are commands that guide the proof search and apply proof steps automatically.
  • Isabelle focuses on declarative proofs. Users typically write proofs as structured proof scripts using Isabelle's proof language, which provides a higher-level representation of proof steps and reasoning principles.

Proof Automation:

  • Coq offers a robust automation system and a wide range of built-in tactics. It has powerful automated proof procedures, such as decision procedures and tactics for handling arithmetic, that can significantly reduce the manual effort required for proving certain properties.
  • Isabelle also provides automation support, but it places more emphasis on interactive proof development. It offers proof methods and tools, such as simplifiers and proof search strategies, but the user is generally more involved in guiding and constructing the proofs.

Community and Ecosystem:

  • Coq has a large and active community with a rich ecosystem of libraries and developments. It is widely used in both academia and industry, particularly in areas like formal verification of software and hardware.
  • Isabelle also has a strong community and is widely used in academia and industry. It has a large collection of theories and libraries built on top of its logical foundation, making it suitable for various application domains.

Choosing between Coq and Isabelle often depends on the specific requirements of the project, the preferred proof style, and the available expertise within a given community.
