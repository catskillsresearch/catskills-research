The language ("tragic gap," "epitaph") evokes irreversible loss, suggesting courts can only document failures rather than prevent them. This reflects frustration with executive noncompliance (e.g., ignoring reinstatement orders or defunding judicial mechanisms).

2. Challenging Core

The subtext is a call to action:

  • Judicial defiance: Judges use rulings to expose executive overreach, creating a public record of constitutional violations (e.g., NLRB v. SW General or Arpaio v. Obama).

  • Moral urgency: Terms like "tragic gap" frame executive actions as moral failures, rallying public and institutional pushback.

  • Accountability: By refusing stays, judges force executives to either comply openly (e.g., reinstating fired workers) or reveal contempt for the law.

The prose acknowledges enforcement limits: Courts lack armies or budgets, so their power hinges on societal trust. If executives erode that trust (e.g., by weaponizing "states’ rights" to defy federal rulings), the law’s authority collapses—hence the "epitaph" metaphor.
