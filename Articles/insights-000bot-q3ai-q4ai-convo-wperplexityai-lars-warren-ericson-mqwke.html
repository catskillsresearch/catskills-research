Can anybody purchase access to Twitter API? They used to be quite restrictive about social science research applications. All changed under Musk?

Under Elon Musk's leadership, Twitter's API access policy has undergone significant changes, making it more accessible but also more expensive for researchers and developers[1][6].

Changes to API Access

1. Elimination of Free Tier: In February 2023, Twitter announced the removal of its free API access[4][5].

2. Introduction of Paid Tiers: Twitter replaced its previous API access tiers with new Free and Basic tiers[9].

3. Basic Tier Pricing: The Basic tier, which provides read access to user profiles and tweets, costs $100 per month[9].

4. Reduced Data Access: The Basic tier offers significantly less data access compared to previous tiers, allowing only 10,000 tweets per month versus 500,000 on the former Essential tier[9].

Impact on Research

1. Academic Exodus: The changes have led to a significant reduction in academic research using Twitter data[7].

2. Increased Costs: Some researchers report that enterprise API access now costs around $42,000 per month, making it prohibitively expensive for many academic institutions[6].

3. Limited Functionality: The Free tier only allows posting tweets and accessing authorized user profiles, severely limiting its usefulness for research[9].

Accessibility

While the new policy theoretically allows anyone to purchase API access, the high costs and reduced functionality have effectively made it less accessible for many researchers and small-scale developers[10].

Contrast with Previous Policy

Previously, Twitter was known for its generous API access, particularly for academic research[3]. The platform offered free access to millions of tweets per day for researchers, which is no longer the case[2].

In conclusion, while API access is now technically available for purchase to anyone, the changes under Musk have made it significantly more challenging and expensive for researchers and developers to access and utilize Twitter data compared to the platform's previous policies[1][6][7].

What about your WhatsApps, Telegrams, RedNotes and the like, the foreign and woolier ones, do they have APIs or are they otherwise scrapable?

When it comes to messaging and social media platforms like WhatsApp, Telegram, and RedNote, the availability of APIs and scrapability varies:

WhatsApp

WhatsApp has a Business API that allows limited access for business accounts, but it's quite restricted:

- Only available to approved businesses through official partners

- Limited to sending messages to users who have opted in

- Does not allow bulk messaging or accessing user data

Scraping WhatsApp is against their terms of service and technically challenging due to end-to-end encryption.

Telegram

Telegram offers more open access through its API:

- Official Bot API for creating chatbots and automated services

- MTProto API for building custom Telegram clients

- Libraries like Telethon and Pyrogram simplify API usage

While scraping public channels is possible, accessing private chats requires user authentication and may violate terms of service.

RedNote (Xiaohongshu)

RedNote, the Chinese social media platform, does not offer a public API. Scraping RedNote faces several challenges:

- No official API for developers

- Terms of service prohibit unauthorized data collection

- Chinese government regulations may restrict data access

Some third-party services claim to offer RedNote scraping, but their legality and reliability are questionable.

Ethical and Legal Considerations

For all these platforms, consider the following when attempting to access or scrape data:

- Respect user privacy and data protection laws

- Adhere to each platform's terms of service

- Be aware of potential legal risks, especially with international platforms

- Consider the ethical implications of data collection and usage

In general, while some of these platforms offer limited API access, widespread scraping is often against their policies and may pose legal and ethical challenges. It's crucial to approach data collection from these sources with caution and in compliance with applicable regulations.

In summary, gathering public sentiment is a complex, expensive hassle.

You're correct that gathering public sentiment, especially on geopolitical and conflict issues in near real-time, is indeed a complex and often expensive endeavor. The landscape of available data sources and tools presents several challenges:

  1. Access Restrictions: Many major social media platforms like Twitter have significantly tightened their API access, making it costly or difficult for researchers and developers to gather data.

  2. Cost Barriers: Platforms that do offer API access often charge substantial fees, putting them out of reach for many individual researchers or smaller organizations.

  3. Data Quality and Representativeness: Even when data is accessible, questions arise about how representative it is of broader public sentiment, given the varying user bases of different platforms.

  4. Technical Hurdles: Aggregating data from multiple sources often requires significant technical expertise and resources.

  5. Ethical and Legal Considerations: Data collection must navigate complex ethical and legal landscapes, especially when dealing with international sources.

  6. Rapid Changes: The landscape of available tools and platforms is constantly shifting, requiring ongoing adaptation of data collection strategies.

While there are some promising alternatives like Mastodon and specialized platforms like GDELT, each comes with its own limitations in terms of user base, data scope, or accessibility. The lack of a single, comprehensive, and affordable solution for real-time public sentiment analysis remains a significant challenge in the field.
