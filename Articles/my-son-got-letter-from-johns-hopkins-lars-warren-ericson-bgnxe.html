My son is a rising junior in high school and has been getting a lot of mail from colleges lately. My wife's favorite is the one from Columbia, where she went to grad school. My favorite is the one from Johns Hopkins, because I know 3 people from Johns Hopkins, and they are all absolutely at the top of their game. Johns Hopkins also claims to get more research dollars than anybody.

In fiscal year 2021, Johns Hopkins spent a record $3.181 billion on research and development, far surpassing the second-place institution, the University of California, San Francisco, which spent $1.7 billion.

I presume this is biased towards biomedical but I don't know the whole picture.

Perplexity AI: Johns Hopkins has been the top university in research spending for over four decades, largely driven by its renowned medical school and Applied Physics Laboratory. In fiscal year 2021, Johns Hopkins' research expenditures totaled $3.181 billion, with the Applied Physics Laboratory accounting for $1.949 billion of that amount. The university first crossed the $2 billion threshold in research spending in fiscal year 2010 and remains the only institution to have done so. A significant portion of Johns Hopkins' research funding comes from federal agencies like the National Institutes of Health, National Science Foundation, Department of Defense, and NASA. While Johns Hopkins leads in overall research expenditures, it also tops the rankings for spending on federally funded research, becoming the first university to surpass $2 billion in that category in fiscal year 2016.

So...doctor stuff and whatever Applied Physics is. One of the three people I know there does crowd-computer hybrid geopolitical forecasting, and that is called Applied Physics, so I dunno really. It is definitely a campus with a research focus which engages undergrads in the work, and that's what I want for my son. So I hope he gets in there, when the time comes. I've never been there.

I did spend quite a bit of time over the years in and around Columbia. I don't feel it, really. It's kind of the coldest and most urban of the Ivy Leagues.

Columbia doesn't really have that exclusive, posh gold-leaf feel you get from a Princeton or a Harvard.

I guess Columbia is just as good as the other Ivy Leagues, but it lacks the exclusive, tweed-jacket Deep State vibe of the others.

I'm Team Johns Hopkins.

The letter directed my son to a web link which was a kind of pre-pre-pre-registration for the college. This started with a personality test. They asked 5 questions, with multiple non-exclusive alternatives, on various activities and situations. In the end, they send you a diagnosis (it is a medical school after all). This was my son's:

Your Hopkins character is...Jay the Blue Jay! In addition to leading our athletic teams to victory, our mascot Jay represents enthusiastic support. He actively spreads cheer and is at his best when people feel excited. Jay is that friend who loves listening to you nerd out about your hobbies, research, or projects. He’s happy to bounce ideas around with you and come up with solutions together but is just as content to simply lend an ear. Like Jay, you motivate everyone around you and thrive when you’re surrounded by passionate people. With 24 varsity teams and 40+ intramurals at Hopkins, you've got your pick of teams to join. You also understand there’s a time to lead, and there’s a time to support. If you’re not on the field or court, then you’re in the stands cheering loudly with The Flock or shouting the fight song with Pep Band.

OK, that's good to know. The diagnosis leads to another part of the pre-pre-pre-application. They want to know if you are a first-generation college student. (No, by a long shot.) Gender (my son is Male). Race (Optional). What to say there? Half European, a quarter Colombian, a quarter Iranian, what is that? Hispanic? Middle Eastern? WASP? Hard to say. Primary academic interest: Engineering, humanities, natural sciences, social sciences? Non-exclusive choices. He picked engineering, which is fair. But I don't want him to prejudge or exclude natural sciences. He won't be able to at Johns Hopkins, I don't think. It's there. Hard to avoid. But if he wants to build rockets, I'm all for that. I don't know where Johns Hopkins stands on the rocket-building. Maybe not so much. That's a tough one.

Then there's this question:

3 choices. That's it. So: you go to Johns Hopkins to become a lawyer, a doctor, or run a startup? That is how I read it. Which is OK! They have $500K funds for student startups! Why not! My son does want to make money. Although my attempts to get him to learn Pandas (for stock trading) off of Udemy have been a bit frustrating. The lecturer has a boring Swedish accent, speaks very slowly, and talks down to the viewer. In spite of the enticement of the Bourse, my son would rather spend his summer vacation time playing video games than master the hierarchical indexing of dataframes. Which, to be fair, Pandas is the most R-like monstrosity in Python, hard to love, but eventually, hard to do without.

This raises a question: Will my son be able to find himself at Johns Hopkins as, say, as a molecular biologist, if he doesn't want to do a pharma startup? Especially after I explained the survival probability of pharma startups to him during a car ride last weekend?

My PhD thesis (at NYU) had a logic theme. I wouldn't call myself a logician. I am surrounded by Physics majors where I work now, mostly to my disadvantage. I studied mostly the wrong stuff for my eventual de facto profession. It worries me. Business. Pre-Health. Pre-Law. Feels limiting. Doesn't feel like there is room there to test the waters, for my son to find his calling with ample information. I know he hasn't found his calling yet. He's good at stuff. He's a good student. But if you ask him what he wants to be when he grows up, he doesn't have anything like a ready answer. He's too smart to not end up doing something smart. He just doesn't know what it is yet.

This puts him at a distinct disadvantage for college applications, which worries me. He's 16, and he's finished college Calc 2 already. Not AP Calculus. Actual college Calc 2, at an actual college. But he's not a proper college student. He's just in a situation where he can do that. But if he allowed himself to go off the deep end on the extracurriculars (for example, putting all of his time into his FIRST Robotics team, which failed to go to Houston and then broke up in a spectacular personality clash between some parents and the full-time mentors), he wouldn't have notched his A's in Calc 1 and Calc 2.

He did good stuff on the Robotics team: A lot of parts fabrication and shop work and CAD. Not stuff to write home about though. And he cut back when his coursework got hard. I have been very careful not to let him overcommit himself. But the lack of an early determination on his direction, and lack of a list of organized competition trophies and medals to point to: it's not enough for a lot of applications. Time will tell how Johns Hopkins reads him. I'm really hoping to find my son a college that will see the solid skill building in his completed coursework, and the open mind behind the lack of direction. Applications are very competitive and many are carefully crafted with expensive advice. His will be home-made and without guile. I really hope the Admissions Department of the right school will see the solid player that he is, so he can find the resources, guidance and good company to excel.
