Steps Involved

- Denmark assesses the situation and determines that U.S. actions constitute an armed attack on Greenland.

- The Danish government formally requests NATO's intervention under Article 5, citing the need for collective defense.

2. NATO Council Meeting:

- A special meeting of the North Atlantic Council is convened, where representatives from all NATO member states discuss the situation.

- The council deliberates on whether U.S. actions indeed qualify as an attack under Article 5.

3. Invocation of Article 5:

- If a consensus is reached that U.S. actions are aggressive and violate NATO principles, Article 5 is invoked.

- The invocation signifies that an attack on Greenland is considered an attack on all NATO allies, obligating them to respond.

4. Military Planning and Coordination:

- NATO military planners develop a response strategy, which may include deploying forces to Greenland and surrounding areas.

- Coordination occurs between various NATO commands, including maritime and air forces, to ensure readiness for a collective response.

5. Mobilization of Forces:

- NATO member states mobilize their military forces in preparation for potential engagement.

- This may involve air deployments, naval assets in the North Atlantic, and ground forces positioned in Europe for rapid response.

6. Diplomatic Efforts:

- Concurrently, diplomatic channels are utilized in an attempt to de-escalate the situation.

- NATO leaders reach out to U.S. officials to negotiate a peaceful resolution and withdrawal of U.S. forces from Greenland.

7. Engagement Decision:

- If diplomatic efforts fail and U.S. military presence escalates or becomes hostile, NATO may decide on a military engagement plan.

- This could include airstrikes against U.S. military installations in Greenland or surrounding areas to neutralize threats.

8. Execution of Military Action:

- NATO launches coordinated military operations aimed at defending Greenland and deterring further U.S. aggression.

- Member states execute their respective roles based on pre-established plans and capabilities.

9. Post-Conflict Resolution:

- Following military engagement, efforts focus on restoring stability in Greenland.

- A framework for future governance and security arrangements is established involving international oversight to prevent further conflict.

Conclusion

This scenario underscores the complexities involved in invoking Article 5 within NATO, especially when actions involve a member state like the United States. It highlights the balance between military readiness and diplomatic resolution in maintaining alliance cohesion while addressing aggressive actions against a member's territory.
