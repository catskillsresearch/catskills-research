What is a G-SIB?

Perplexity. Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) are banks identified based on a scoring system developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS). Banks with scores above a specific threshold are classified as G-SIBs and allocated to different buckets that determine their higher loss absorbency requirements.

The G-SIB framework aims to reduce the likelihood and severity of the failure of globally important financial institutions. The methodology relies on an indicator-based quantitative approach that considers factors like size, interconnectedness, lack of substitutes, global activity, and complexity to assess systemic importance. G-SIBs are subject to stricter prudential regulations, including higher capital requirements and additional surcharges.

The G-SIB assessment is conducted annually, with banks potentially moving between buckets based on changes in their systemic importance relative to other banks in the sample. The higher loss absorbency requirements for G-SIBs start at 1.0% of risk-weighted assets for the lowest bucket and increase up to 3.5% for the top bucket.
